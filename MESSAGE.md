# DAB — Outreach Message Standard

## Purpose
Introduce DAB's simple, low-maintenance Google review invitation service to relevant local businesses. The goal is an honest, low-pressure conversation, not a lengthy sales presentation.

## Recipient qualification — mandatory
- Research the business, active location, and current industry. Prefer independent businesses with regular customer handovers and an observable gap in **recent** genuine Google reviews versus relevant local peers.
- **Use only a verified named decision-maker or relevant manager and their direct, publicly available work email.** Verify name, role and email from the business's official channels or another reliable public business source. A name on a website does **not** authorize guessing their email format.
- **Do not draft to generic or shared role inboxes**, including addresses whose local part is `info`, `contact`, `hello`, `office`, `admin`, `reception`, `service`, `support`, `sales`, `team`, `mail`, `enquiries`, `inquiries`, `booking`, `bookings`, `appointments`, `praxis`, or similar. This includes variants like `info.uk@` or `contact-branch@`. Do not substitute contact forms to evade this restriction.
- A generic address is **not proof** an email would go to spam; this is a deliberate commercial targeting preference.
- Check accessible records for prior opt-outs and avoid repeat contacts. Within each run deduplicate by normalized address and business identity. Never claim a recipient is safe if this cannot be verified.
- Do not draft to people or businesses for which the outreach would be inappropriate or unsupported.

## The email — default rules
- Approximately **75–115 words** for the body (excluding sign-off); one simple idea and one low-friction question.
- Natural language of the recipient's market, grammatically fluent, locally appropriate, professional but not corporate.
- Start with **one genuinely researched, neutral or positive, specific observation**. The observation must be true and current. Avoid opening by scolding the owner about weak review counts.
- Explain the product plainly: after an eligible completed service, an employee enters a customer's phone number **with the necessary permission** and DAB sends an SMS with a direct link where the customer may leave an **honest Google review**.
- No exaggerated promises, fabricated customer stories, guaranteed review counts or ranking improvements. No review gating; invitations must not be restricted to customers presumed likely to leave positive reviews.
- **Do not mention price in the first email by default.** If the user supplies and explicitly requests a price, use that exact price and currency; never make up a price. Do not promise a trial unless the user has approved the offer.
- Use straightforward, truthful subject lines; avoid clickbait, negative shaming, and attention tricks.
- One low-pressure call to action: offer a brief demonstration or explanation.
- Sign `Michael\nDAB` unless the user supplies different approved sender details. Always identify the actual business/sender accurately.
- Do not reuse large identical paragraphs across prospects. Differences should come from genuine industry and business context, not meaningless synonym rotation to evade filters.
- Draft only. Never send without a separate explicit instruction.

## Example — English, independent garage
Subject: A quick idea for [Garage Name]

Hi [First Name],

I came across [Garage Name] while looking at independent garages in [Town]. [One short, verified observation relevant to this garage.]

I run DAB, a simple service that helps businesses invite customers to leave genuine Google reviews. After a job, a staff member enters the customer's number with permission, and the customer receives a text with a direct review link.

I handle the setup, so there's very little for your team to learn.

Would you like a quick look at how it works?

Best,
Michael
DAB

This is a structure reference, **not** a message to paste verbatim or a license to invent bracketed facts.

## Optional evidence
A customer reported 4 new reviews from 28 SMS review invitations in one week. Cite this only if permission to use the result is available and attribution is accurate. Label it as one customer's experience, not a typical or guaranteed outcome.

## Campaign learning
Keep these questions separate:
- Did the message fail to reach the recipient (bounce or confirmed test)?
- Did it reach an inbox but get ignored? (Not directly observable from silence.)
- Did a relevant decision-maker express interest?
- Did the prospect become a paying customer and remain subscribed?

Never diagnose spam placement solely from nonresponse; don't generalize all businesses in a country from one campaign.
